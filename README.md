# Steam — Improving Game Discovery

## Overview

**Product:** Steam (Valve Corporation)  
**Project Type:** Product Management Case Study  
**Focus Area:** Game Discovery, Personalization & User Engagement

Steam offers a massive library of games, but finding something interesting to play can sometimes feel overwhelming. Users may encounter repetitive recommendations, struggle to discover lesser-known games, or rely on external platforms and friends to decide what to play.

This project explores how Steam could make game discovery more focused, personalized, and engaging.

## Goal

Improve Steam engagement by making it easier for users to discover relevant, interesting games they actually want to play.

## Problem Statement

Users can struggle to find games they want to play because of information overload, repetitive recommendations, limited visibility of lesser-known titles, and uncertainty about whether a game matches their interests or system requirements.

## Research Approach

I conducted quantitative and qualitative research to understand users' gaming habits, discovery methods, and challenges.

- **User surveys:** Gathered insights into game discovery behavior, recommendation preferences, and purchase decisions.
- **User interviews:** Conducted three live interviews to explore real gaming experiences, frustrations, and decision-making patterns.
- **Behavioral analysis:** Examined how users choose games based on mood, available time, friends' activity, price, reviews, and hardware compatibility.
- **Problem validation:** Compared research findings to identify recurring pain points and potential opportunities.

## Key Research Insights

- **External discovery matters:** Users frequently discover indie and lesser-known games through YouTube, Instagram, streamers, and other external platforms.
- **Information overload:** Steam's interface and extensive catalogue can make discovery feel overwhelming.
- **Repetitive recommendations:** Users may encounter games that feel too similar to what they already know.
- **Mood influences game selection:** Users choose different experiences depending on whether they want to relax, compete, or enjoy a story.
- **Purchase uncertainty:** Price, reviews, player count, and system requirements influence purchase decisions.
- **Social influence:** Friends' recommendations and multiplayer availability can affect what users decide to play.

## Target Users

The project focuses on **Discovery Seekers** — users who want to find interesting games but may struggle to decide what to play or discover titles beyond their usual preferences.

Their needs include:
- Finding games that match their current mood or intent.
- Discovering quality indie and lesser-known titles.
- Exploring games beyond their usual genres.
- Understanding why a game is being recommended.
- Reducing the effort required to find something interesting.

## Proposed Solution: Discovery Mode

**Discovery Mode** is a proposed, optional discovery experience within Steam that helps users find something interesting to play without endlessly browsing the store.

Unlike regular recommendations, which primarily answer *“What games might you like?”*, Discovery Mode focuses on *“Help me find something interesting to play right now.”*

### Core Features

**1. Mood-Based Discovery**
**2. Different From Your Usual**
**3. Indie & Hidden Gems**
**4. Short Discovery Cards**
**5. Why This Game?**
**6. Reduce Home Screen Noise**

## Product Prioritization

I explored multiple potential solutions and used **RICE prioritization** to compare opportunities based on Reach, Impact, Confidence, and Effort.

Rather than selecting a solution solely because it received the highest numerical score, I also considered the underlying user problem, research evidence, and alignment with the product goal.

This helped distinguish solutions that address root causes from those that only improve individual symptoms.

## Success Metrics

The primary objective is to improve meaningful engagement with game discovery.

Potential success metrics include:
- **Discovery engagement:** Increase interactions with recommended and discovered games.
- **Game exploration:** Increase visits to game detail pages from Discovery Mode.
- **Discovery conversion:** Measure how often users wishlist, follow, or purchase discovered games.
- **Discovery satisfaction:** Evaluate whether users find the recommendations relevant and useful.

### Guardrail Metrics

To ensure that increased engagement reflects a better user experience, monitor:
- Recommendation dismissal and skip rates.
- User feedback on recommendation relevance.
- Repetitive or irrelevant recommendation rates.
- Discovery Mode abandonment.

*These are proposed metrics for evaluating the concept, not measured results.*
